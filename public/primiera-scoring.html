What the primiera is

The primiera, or prime, is one of the four fixed points in Scopa, along with cards, Coins and the settebello. It goes to the player who, from the cards captured in the round, puts together the best combination of four cards, one of each suit.

What decides it is not the number printed on the card, but a special value used only for this count.

The card values

In short: 7 = 21, 6 = 18, Ace = 16, 5 = 15, 4 = 14, 3 = 13, 2 = 12, face cards = 10.

The seven is worth the most, followed by the six and the Ace. The face cards are the weakest: in the primiera a two beats a King.

How to work it out

  1. Sort the cards you captured by suit.
  2. In each suit keep only the card with the highest primiera value.
  3. Add up the four values.
  4. Compare your total with the opponent's: the higher one takes the point.

The maximum is 84, with all four sevens. The minimum, with four face cards, is 40.

An example with an ordinary pile:

SuitCards capturedThe bestValue
Coins7, 6, Ace721
CupsKing, Knight, 2212
Swords5, 4515
Clubs3313
Primiera61

Look at the Cups: the two is worth 12 and beats the King and the Knight, which are worth 10. In the Coins the six and the Ace add nothing, because only one card counts in each suit.

A tie

If Giulia has two sevens and two sixes and Marco has the other two sevens and the other two sixes, it ends 78 all. The primiera point goes to nobody.

Special cases

A missing suit

To compete you need at least one card of every suit. A player who captured no card of one suit has no primiera at all, whatever the other suits hold: the point goes to the opponent, provided the opponent has all four suits. If both players are missing a suit, nobody gets the point.

Equal totals

If the totals are equal, the point is not awarded.

The settebello

In the primiera the seven of Coins is worth 21, like the other sevens. The settebello point is a separate point, scored on top.

In Scopa AI the count follows these rules: at the end of the round you see both players' primiera, and the point is awarded automatically.

Four shortcuts

At the table you do not always need to do all the sums.

  • Four sevens. The primiera is yours: 84 cannot be beaten.
  • Three sevens. The opponent can beat you only if your fourth suit holds nothing but face cards, or no card at all.
  • Two sevens each. The sum in the other two suits decides, and the cards that weigh most there are the sixes (18) and the Aces (16).
  • No sevens. The primiera is lost: if you have none, the opponent has all four.

Where the name comes from

The word comes from a much older card game, primiera, known in English as primero. It is recorded from 1526 and was played across Europe for centuries. In that game a “primiera” was a hand made of one card of each suit, and the cards had the same values still used in Scopa today: 21 for a seven, 18 for a six, 16 for an Ace.

Frequently asked questions

What is a seven worth in the primiera?

21, the highest value. That goes for all four sevens, the settebello included.

What are the Knave, Knight and King worth?

10 each: less than every other card, the two included.

How many cards count towards the primiera?

Four, the best one of each suit. The other cards you captured add nothing.

Who gets the point if the primiera is tied?

Nobody: the point is not awarded.

Can you win the primiera with a single seven?

Yes. One seven and three sixes make 75, more than three sevens and a face card, which stop at 73.

And with no sevens?

No. If you have no seven, the opponent has all four and scores 84, the maximum.

What happens if I have no card of one suit?

You have no primiera and the point goes to the opponent, if the opponent has all four suits.
