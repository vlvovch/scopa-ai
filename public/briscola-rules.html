Briscola is a trick-taking game: in each trick the players lay down one card each, and the stronger card takes both. One suit, the briscola or trump suit, beats all the others. The origins of the game are unclear; it is recorded from the first half of the 19th century.

This page explains classic two-player Briscola, with the same rules you will find in Briscola AI.

The card values

Briscola is played with an Italian deck of 40 cards in four suits: Coins, Cups, Swords and Clubs. Each suit runs from the Ace to the seven, plus three face cards: the Knave (fante), the Knight (cavallo) and the King (re). Five cards in each suit score points; the other five are worth nothing.

The ranking in tricks is the same: Ace, three, King, Knight, Knave, then 7, 6, 5, 4 and 2. Mind the three: it is the second strongest card and beats the King.

Aces and threes are called carichi, “loads”; the cards that score nothing are lisci, “plain” cards. Each suit is worth 30 points, the whole deck 120.

The deal and the trump suit

The dealer gives three cards to each player, then turns the next card face up and tucks it under the deck so that it stays visible: its suit is the briscola, the trump suit, for the whole round. The player who did not deal leads first.

How a trick is played

The player on lead lays down a card and the other answers with any card: in Briscola there is no obligation to follow suit, or to play a trump.

Who wins the trick?

  • If a trump was played, the trump wins. With two trumps, the stronger one wins.
  • If both cards are of the same suit, the stronger one wins.
  • If they are of different suits and neither is a trump, the first card played wins.

The winner takes both cards and puts them face down on their pile. The winner then draws a card from the deck, followed by the opponent, and leads to the next trick.

The last tricks

When a single face-down card is left in the deck, the winner of the trick draws it and the opponent takes the face-up trump card. From then on the last three tricks are played without drawing.

There are twenty tricks in all.

Scoring

When the cards run out, each player adds up the points in their pile. There are 120 at stake: the round goes to whoever scores at least 61. At 60 points each the round is tied.

By tradition the points are counted only at the end. During the round the piles stay face down, and remembering which cards have gone is part of the game.

In Briscola AI a round that ends 60 to 60 counts for neither player: another one is played.

The match

You can play a single round, or a match over several rounds: the winner is the first to win the agreed number of rounds. The deal changes hands every round.

Tips for beginners

  • Playing second is an advantage. You see the opponent's card before you choose: that is the moment to cash in a carico or to spend a trump on a sure thing.
  • On lead, risk little. Leading a liscio gives away no points. A carico led first can be taken with a trump.
  • Count the trumps. There are ten: knowing which have gone, starting with the Ace and the three, tells you what the ones in your hand are worth.
  • Count the points. There are 120 in all: once you reach 61 the round is yours.

Variants

Briscola is played in other ways too. These are the best-known variants; none of them is in the app, which follows the classic rules described above.

  • Four players. Two pairs play, and partners add their points together.
  • Three players. One two is removed from the deck, so that the cards divide evenly.
  • Briscola chiamata. Played by five.
  • Briscola scoperta. For two, with the hands face up.

Frequently asked questions

What is the three worth in Briscola?

10 points. It is the second strongest card of its suit, after the Ace, which is worth 11.

Do you have to follow suit?

No. In every trick you can play any card from your hand.

How many points do you need to win?

At least 61 of the 120 at stake.

What happens if it ends 60 to 60?

The round is tied. In Briscola AI it counts for neither player and another one is played.

How many cards are dealt in Briscola?

Three each. After every trick each player draws a card, until the deck runs out.

Who draws first?

The winner of the trick, who also leads to the next one.

Who gets the face-up trump card?

The loser of the trick after which the last cards are drawn: the winner takes the last face-down card, the other player the trump card.

What are carichi and lisci?

The carichi are the Ace and the three, the cards worth the most. The lisci are the cards worth nothing: seven, six, five, four and two.
